# MoDojo Global Website Design

## 1. Brand Identity

MoDojo is a **premium business management platform** designed to make complex studio operations feel simple.

The brand sits between:

**Premium SaaS**
+
**Modern wellness / lifestyle**
+
**Friendly business software**

It should not look like traditional enterprise software.

It should also not look like a generic fitness application.

### The desired feeling

When someone first visits the website, they should think:

> **"This looks professional."**

Then:

> **"This looks easy to use."**

Then:

> **"This could make running my business much easier."**

---

# 2. Core Brand Message

The website should consistently reinforce:

### **Studio management, made seamless.**

Supporting ideas:

* Less administrative work
* Everything connected
* Easier daily operations
* Better client experiences
* Better business visibility
* More time to focus on the business

Avoid positioning MoDojo as simply:

> "A software with lots of features."

Instead position it as:

> **"A simpler way to run your business."**

---

# 3. Overall Design Vibe

The entire website should feel:

* Modern
* Premium
* Calm
* Spacious
* Fluid
* Sophisticated
* Human
* Confident
* Approachable

### Visual reference

Think:

**Premium SaaS website**

rather than:

**Traditional business management software**

And:

**Modern wellness brand**

rather than:

**Generic fitness app**

---

# 4. Design Principle — Fluid, Not Rigid

The website should feel like a continuous experience.

Avoid building every section as a rigid rectangular block.

Do not repeatedly use:

```text
┌──────────────────────────────┐
│ Heading                      │
│                              │
│ Card  Card  Card             │
│                              │
└──────────────────────────────┘
```

Instead use:

* Asymmetrical compositions
* Curved section boundaries
* Large typography
* Overlapping screenshots
* Floating elements
* Generous whitespace
* Different content widths
* Organic visual transitions

Sections should visually connect to one another.

---

# 5. Color Philosophy

MoDojo should have a **strong navy identity**.

Do not make the website predominantly white with tiny navy accents.

Use navy as a recurring visual anchor.

### Primary Navy

`#121D3C`

Use for:

* Hero
* Major feature sections
* CTA sections
* Navigation
* Footer
* Primary buttons
* Important headlines

### Deep Navy

`#00041A`

Use for:

* Footer
* Very dark sections
* High-contrast areas

### Secondary Navy

`#1A146B`

Use sparingly for:

* Supporting UI
* Secondary visual elements
* Small accents

### Orange

`#F67D38`

Orange should be an **accent**, not the primary brand color.

Use for:

* Primary CTA buttons
* Small highlights
* Important words
* Icons
* Interactive states

### Light Gray

`#F1F5F9`

Use as the main soft background.

### Soft Lavender

`#F5F6FF`

Use for:

* Cards
* Supporting backgrounds
* Secondary content

---

# 6. Color Balance

Recommended approximate visual balance:

**40% White / Light backgrounds**

**30% Navy**

**20% Soft gray / lavender**

**10% Orange and other accents**

Do not use orange as a large background.

The website should remain sophisticated and calm.

---

# 7. Typography

Use **Plus Jakarta Sans** throughout the website.

Typography should feel:

* Modern
* Rounded
* Confident
* Highly readable

Large headings should be visually dominant.

Example:

> **Studio management,**
> **made seamless.**

Use relatively short headlines.

Avoid long paragraphs directly underneath large headings.

---

# 8. Headline Style

Headlines should communicate **benefits**, not technical descriptions.

Prefer:

> **Spend less time managing.**

over:

> **A comprehensive management system for your business.**

Prefer:

> **Everything in one place.**

over:

> **Integrated business management functionality.**

Prefer:

> **Give your clients a seamless experience.**

over:

> **Advanced member management capabilities.**

---

# 9. Navigation

The navigation should be minimal and premium.

### Desktop

```text

About Us
Product Features
Pricing
Contact Us

Sign In
Start Free Trial
```

The navigation should have generous spacing.

Avoid cramming too many links into the header.

### Header behavior

At the top of the page:

**Transparent / integrated with hero**

After scrolling:

**White or lightly translucent navigation**

Use subtle blur if appropriate.

The navigation should remain sticky.

---

# 12. Buttons — Interaction

Hover states should be subtle.

Do not use dramatic transformations.

Use:

* Slight darkening/lightening
* Small upward movement
* Soft shadow
* Smooth transition

Animation duration:

Approximately `150–250ms`.

---

# 13. Cards

Cards should feel **soft and premium**.

Default:

* White or `#F5F6FF`
* Border: `#D7DCEE`
* Radius: `20–28px`
* Minimal shadow
* Generous padding

Avoid:

* Thick borders
* Strong shadows
* Excessive icons
* Tiny cards everywhere

Cards should be used only when they improve organization.

---

# 14. Product UI Showcase

Real MoDojo interfaces should be one of the main visual assets of the website.

Prioritize:

* Dashboard
* Members
* Classes calendar
* Bookings
* Invoices
* Payments
* Member Portal
* Reports

### Presentation

Screenshots should have:

* Large rounded corners
* Soft shadows
* Clean cropping
* Slight depth
* Natural perspective where appropriate

Screenshots can overlap section boundaries.

Example:

```text
        NAVY SECTION

       Headline
       Copy
       CTA

        ┌──────────────────┐
        │                  │
        │   MODOJO UI      │
        │                  │
        └──────────────────┘
                ↓
       ┌──────────────────────┐
       │ NEXT SECTION         │
       └──────────────────────┘
```

The product should feel tangible.

---

# 15. Layout Philosophy

Do not use the same layout repeatedly.

Mix:

### Split layouts

```text
Text                 Screenshot
```

### Reverse split

```text
Screenshot           Text
```

### Centered

```text
          Heading
          Copy

       Large Screenshot
```

### Full-width visual

```text
       Heading

 ┌──────────────────────────────┐
 │                              │
 │       Product Interface      │
 │                              │
 └──────────────────────────────┘
```

### Asymmetrical

```text
             Heading

      Screenshot
                         Supporting
                         information
```

This creates a more editorial and dynamic experience.

---

# 16. Section Transitions

Avoid hard visual cuts between every section.

Use:

* Large rounded containers
* Overlapping content
* Curved edges
* Large spacing
* Background transitions

Example:

```text
██████████████████████████
       NAVY
        Hero

      Product UI
██████████████████████████
          ╭───────────────
          │
          │ LIGHT SECTION
          │
          ╰───────────────
```

The website should feel like one long canvas.

→ **How do I get started?**

* Full comparison table
* Value explanation
* Payment information

# 24. Icons

Use a consistent minimalist icon set.

Icons should be:

* Simple
* Line-based
* Lightweight
* Rounded

Avoid mixing multiple icon styles.

Icons should support the content rather than become the main visual.

---

# 25. Decorative Graphics

Use subtle graphics inspired by:

* Flow
* Connection
* Movement
* Circles
* Paths
* Soft curves

The decoration should reinforce the idea of **seamless connection**.

Avoid:

* Neon glows
* Cyberpunk aesthetics
* Excessive gradients
* Random 3D objects
* Generic technology illustrations

---

# 26. Animation

Animations should feel smooth and intentional.

Use:

* Fade
* Slide
* Gentle scale
* Scroll reveal
* Product screenshot movement
* Hover states
* Number counters

Animation should communicate:

> **Flow**

not:

> **Look at this animation!**

Avoid excessive motion.

Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.

---

# 27. Scroll Experience

The website should feel rewarding to scroll.

Introduce visual changes gradually.

For example:

**Hero**

Large and immersive.

↓

**Problem**

More editorial and human.

↓

**Solution**

Product-focused.

↓

**Product**

Interactive and visual.

↓

**Insights**

Data-driven.

↓

**Testimonials**

Human and emotional.

↓

**CTA**

Strong and simple.

This creates a natural narrative instead of a repetitive sequence of cards.

---

# 28. Responsive Design

The website must be designed responsively from the beginning.

### Desktop

Use:

* Large typography
* Asymmetrical layouts
* Floating screenshots
* Horizontal journeys
* Wide visual compositions

### Tablet

Reduce:

* Font sizes
* Horizontal spacing
* Screenshot sizes

Preserve the visual hierarchy.

### Mobile

Transform rather than simply shrink.

Convert:

**Horizontal → Vertical**

**Mega menu → Mobile navigation**

**Floating elements → Stacked elements**

**Large screenshots → Full-width screenshots**

**4-step journeys → Vertical timelines**

Maintain generous whitespace.

---

# 29. Accessibility

Maintain:

* Strong text contrast
* Visible focus states
* Keyboard navigation
* Accessible buttons
* Accessible form labels
* Alt text for meaningful images
* Reduced-motion support

Do not sacrifice usability for visual effects.

---

# 30. Content Style

MoDojo's writing should be:

**Clear**

**Confident**

**Human**

**Concise**

**Benefit-focused**

Avoid excessive technical terminology.

Instead of:

> "Our integrated multi-module architecture enables comprehensive operational management."

Say:

> **"Everything you need to run your business, all in one place."**

---

# 31. Voice

MoDojo should sound like a knowledgeable partner, not a salesperson shouting at the visitor.

### Use

> Make your day easier.

> Spend less time on admin.

> Everything in one place.

> Give your clients a better experience.

> Know what's happening in your business.

### Avoid

> Revolutionize your business today!!!

> The world's most powerful studio management solution!

> Unlock unprecedented business growth!

The brand should be **confident without being loud**.

---

# 32. Empty Space

Whitespace is an important part of the design.

Do not try to fill every available area.

Large amounts of empty space should be used around:

* Hero headlines
* Major section headings
* Product screenshots
* CTAs
* Testimonials

Whitespace makes the product feel premium.

---

# 33. What the Website Should NOT Feel Like

Avoid making MoDojo look like:

* An admin dashboard
* A banking website
* A generic corporate SaaS template
* A traditional gym website
* A cheap startup landing page
* An overly colorful fitness application
* A documentation website

The **application itself can be information-dense**.

The marketing website should be the opposite:

> **Calm. Clear. Beautiful. Easy to understand.**

---

# 34. Core Visual Rule

Every design decision should reinforce one of these ideas:

### SIMPLE

Running the business feels easier.

### CONNECTED

Everything works together.

### SEAMLESS

The experience flows naturally from one step to another.

### PREMIUM

The product feels trustworthy and professional.

### HUMAN

The technology exists to improve people's experience.

---

# 35. Final Design Statement

The MoDojo website should feel like **a calm, premium journey through a complicated business made simple**.

It should use strong navy sections, generous whitespace, oversized typography, organic layouts, subtle orange accents, and real product interfaces.

The website should not overwhelm visitors with features.

Instead, it should make them gradually realize:

> **"MoDojo takes all the complicated parts of running my business and brings them together."**

And ultimately:

> **Studio management, made seamless.**
