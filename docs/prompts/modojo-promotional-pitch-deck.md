# Prompt: Generate the Modojo Promotional Pitch Deck

You are an expert presentation strategist, startup storyteller, and PowerPoint designer. Create a polished promotional pitch deck for **Modojo**, a studio and membership management platform for fitness, wellness, and class-based businesses.

The deck should work in two contexts:

1. A sales presentation for prospective studio owners and managers.
2. A Shark Tank-style startup pitch for partners, investors, or internal leadership.

The deck must be persuasive, visually distinctive, and grounded in the product facts below. Do not make the presentation feel like a generic enterprise SaaS deck.

---

## Product context

### Product

Modojo is an all-in-one operating platform for studios and class-based businesses. It helps businesses manage:

- leads and trial bookings
- members and staff
- classes, schedules, rooms, and capacity
- bookings and waitlists
- attendance and check-in
- memberships and class packages
- invoices, payments, and renewals
- WhatsApp reminders and member communication
- promotions and reports
- multiple branches and role-based access

### Target customers

The primary customer is the studio owner or class-business owner. Relevant segments include:

- fitness studios
- yoga studios
- pilates studios
- boutique fitness studios
- dance studios
- martial arts gyms
- boxing and Muay Thai gyms
- personal training studios
- wellness studios
- small gyms with recurring classes
- multi-branch studio operators

### Primary users

1. Owner / Studio Manager
2. Admin / Front Desk Staff
3. Instructor / Trainer
4. Member

### Core problem

Many studios still rely on disconnected tools such as spreadsheets, WhatsApp groups, Google Calendar, paper attendance lists, and manual bank-transfer tracking. This creates:

- scattered member data
- manual booking and cancellation work
- overbooked or under-filled classes
- difficult capacity and waitlist management
- missed membership renewals
- slow payment follow-up
- unclear revenue and attendance visibility
- limited instructor access to schedules and rosters
- a frustrating member experience

### Core promise

> Manage bookings, memberships, payments, attendance, and member communication in one place.

### Product positioning

> Modojo is the operating system for fitness, wellness, and class-based studios.

### Product direction

Modojo is Indonesia-first and designed to support WhatsApp communication, bank-transfer tracking, QRIS/payment-gateway readiness, class packages, drop-in sessions, membership expiry, and mobile-friendly member access.

Do not claim that every integration or payment capability is already live. Clearly label future, planned, or readiness-stage capabilities where relevant.

---

## Narrative direction

Build the story around this transformation:

> From scattered admin and manual follow-up to one organized operating rhythm for the whole studio.

The deck should make the audience feel:

- “This understands how my studio actually operates.”
- “This could save my team time every day.”
- “This helps me fill classes, retain members, and see the business clearly.”
- “This is premium and focused, not another bloated back-office tool.”

Use concrete studio moments rather than abstract SaaS language:

- a class reaching capacity
- a member joining a waitlist
- a front-desk admin checking someone in
- an instructor viewing today’s roster
- an owner seeing unpaid invoices and expiring packages
- a member booking from a phone
- a WhatsApp reminder reducing manual follow-up

---

## Required deck structure

Create a 12–14 slide deck. Use concise slide copy and put additional explanation in speaker notes rather than overcrowding the slides.

### Slide 1 — Cover

Headline:

> Run your studio with less admin and more momentum.

Subtitle:

> Modojo — the operating system for fitness, wellness, and class-based studios.

Include a visually compelling Modojo dashboard or studio-operations composition. Add a discreet placeholder for presenter name, company, and date.

### Slide 2 — The daily reality

Show the fragmented tools and manual workflows studio operators deal with:

- spreadsheets
- WhatsApp messages
- paper attendance
- calendar tools
- manual payment tracking

Use a visual “tool sprawl” composition. End with the message:

> The studio is growing. The operating system is not.

### Slide 3 — The cost of fragmentation

Present the operational consequences:

- admin overload
- missed follow-ups
- empty or overbooked classes
- unclear package balances
- late payments
- weak visibility into retention and revenue

Do not invent percentages or financial losses. Use qualitative statements or clearly marked placeholders such as `[insert validated studio metric]`.

### Slide 4 — The Modojo answer

Introduce Modojo as the centralized operating platform.

Use the positioning statement:

> One place to run the member journey — from lead and trial to booking, attendance, payment, and renewal.

Show a simple end-to-end flow:

Lead → Trial → Member → Booking → Check-in → Payment → Renewal

### Slide 5 — One command center for the owner

Show the owner/admin dashboard experience:

- active members
- today’s classes
- occupancy
- revenue
- unpaid invoices
- expiring memberships
- attendance and package usage

Use realistic sample UI data, but label it as **illustrative demo data**. Never present sample numbers as actual traction.

### Slide 6 — Make every class easier to fill

Focus on:

- class schedules
- online booking
- capacity tracking
- waitlists
- cancellations
- recurring classes
- instructor and room assignment

Use a calendar or class-capacity visual with a clear before/after story.

### Slide 7 — Turn attendance into insight

Focus on:

- fast check-in
- instructor rosters
- no-show visibility
- attendance history
- member notes
- retention signals

Show how the owner, instructor, and front desk each see the information they need.

### Slide 8 — Keep memberships and cash flow moving

Focus on:

- memberships and packages
- remaining sessions
- expiry dates
- invoices
- payment status
- renewal reminders
- manual or digital payment workflows

Be precise: describe QRIS and payment-gateway support as “readiness” or “planned integration” unless verified as live.

### Slide 9 — One platform, three experiences

Show the three connected product surfaces:

1. Owner dashboard — visibility and business control
2. Instructor portal — schedule, roster, attendance, and notes
3. Member portal — class discovery, booking, packages, invoices, and contact

Use three distinct but connected visual panels.

### Slide 10 — Built for the way studios work in Indonesia

Highlight the local operating context:

- WhatsApp-first communication
- bank-transfer tracking
- mobile-friendly member access
- class packages and drop-ins
- membership expiry and renewal workflows
- QRIS/payment-gateway readiness
- support for growing and multi-branch studios

Avoid implying regulatory certification or live integrations unless verified.

### Slide 11 — Why Modojo wins

Create a differentiation slide. Compare Modojo with the current alternative, not with named competitors unless verified.

Suggested comparison dimensions:

| Current fragmented workflow | Modojo |
|---|---|
| Separate tools for each task | One connected operating flow |
| Manual booking coordination | Online booking, capacity, and waitlists |
| Paper or scattered attendance | Fast check-in and attendance history |
| Manual renewal follow-up | Package, invoice, and reminder visibility |
| Limited owner visibility | Operational and business dashboards |

Keep the comparison fair and avoid unsupported competitor claims.

### Slide 12 — Business model and go-to-market

Use placeholders where commercial decisions are not yet validated:

- Pricing model: `[insert confirmed pricing model]`
- Typical customer: `[insert confirmed customer profile]`
- Acquisition channels: studio communities, direct sales, partnerships, referrals, and targeted digital marketing
- Expansion path: additional branches, staff workflows, member engagement, payments, and reporting

Do not invent pricing, market size, conversion rates, CAC, LTV, revenue, or customer counts.

### Slide 13 — Traction, roadmap, or proof

If validated data is available, show it clearly with source and date. If it is not available, use a roadmap instead:

- Phase 1: core studio operations
- Next: deeper member engagement and communication
- Next: payment and automation expansion
- Future: multi-branch intelligence and retention insights

Never fabricate testimonials, logos, customer counts, revenue, growth, or pilot results. Use explicit placeholders for missing proof points.

### Slide 14 — Closing ask

For a sales version, use:

> Your studio deserves a better operating rhythm.

CTA:

> Book a Modojo demo

For a Shark Tank/investor version, use:

> Modojo is building the operating system for the next generation of studios.

Ask placeholder:

> `[insert investment or partnership ask]`

End with contact details and a QR-code placeholder if needed.

## Visual design system

Use the existing Modojo visual direction:

- warm neutral background: `#F7F7F5`
- deep ink typography
- primary indigo: `#5B4BDB`
- secondary teal: `#0F766E`
- restrained lime accent only when useful
- Manrope for headlines
- Inter for body text and labels
- rounded cards with approximately 16px corner radius
- generous whitespace
- thin borders and soft shadows
- premium SaaS interface details
- strong light/dark contrast between content sections

Use dark indigo or deep ink for the opening and closing slides. Use light warm-neutral backgrounds for most content slides. Repeat one recognizable visual motif throughout the deck, such as rounded dashboard panels with small indigo status indicators.

The style should feel like premium boutique-studio software: energetic, organized, trustworthy, and human. Avoid generic corporate templates, excessive gradients, stock-photo collages, dense bullet lists, and noisy dashboards.

## Visual asset requirements

Every slide must contain a meaningful visual element:

- product UI mockup
- process flow
- comparison diagram
- metric callout
- calendar or roster visual
- icon system
- roadmap
- simple chart
- studio-related image or illustration

Use generated or licensed visuals only. Do not use copyrighted logos or unlicensed studio photography. If real product screenshots are unavailable, create clearly labeled illustrative UI mockups.

Use realistic examples such as:

- “Morning Yoga”
- “Reformer Pilates Beginner”
- “HIIT Class”
- “Muay Thai Beginner”
- “Boxing Conditioning”

Use Indonesian rupiah formatting only for clearly labeled sample/demo data, and never imply that those figures are actual Modojo business results.

## Presentation requirements

- Output a `.pptx` file.
- Also export a PDF preview if the environment supports it.
- Include speaker notes for every slide.
- Include a short presenter script for the sales version and an alternative investor/Shark Tank script where the narrative differs.
- Keep slide text readable from a distance.
- Use at least 36pt for slide titles, 18–24pt for section headers, and 14–18pt for body text where possible.
- Keep all content inside safe margins.
- Use consistent alignment, spacing, typography, and icon style.
- Make the deck suitable for a 7–10 minute presentation.
- Include a final appendix only if needed for product scope, roadmap, assumptions, or validation notes.

Before finalizing, perform content and visual QA:

1. Verify that all slide text is present and correctly ordered.
2. Check for spelling, punctuation, and terminology consistency.
3. Confirm that no unsupported metrics or claims were invented.
4. Render the slides to images and inspect every slide.
5. Fix overlapping elements, clipping, unreadable text, inconsistent margins, low contrast, and excessive density.
6. Re-render and re-check after making fixes.
7. Report which claims are sourced product facts, which are illustrative examples, and which remain placeholders.

Use product terminology consistently: `member`, `class`, `package`, `check in`, `remaining sessions`, `waitlist`, `invoice`, and `renewal`.

The final presentation should make Modojo feel like a focused, modern, Indonesia-first operating system that helps studio owners spend less time coordinating tools and more time growing their community.
